# Comm Control v6.3

Open [USC campus](http://127.0.0.1:8765/control?world=usc) or [Downtown LA](http://127.0.0.1:8765/control?world=city). The existing Mac launcher connects a fresh browser if needed.

Choose the operating workspace in the header. Each has its own reports, alerts, queue order, reporting sessions and resources. The choice follows navigation, reload and browser history. Switching workspaces keeps your reports intact. Old reports are classified from their saved interpreted location without changing the original sources.

The map is the main working surface. The incident title and location stay visible; open **Report details** for the category, times, uncertainty and related sources. Search floats over the city map. USC's building selector and layer controls float over its custom plan. Wheel or trackpad scrolling over either map zooms at the pointer. Scroll outside the map, or focus the middle panel and use Page Up/Down, to move through long report details. The map also has zoom buttons and keyboard controls. **Expand map** gives it more room; **Restore workspace** or Escape returns to the three panels.

Use **Load USC demo** in an empty USC workspace to add six simulated facilities reports without deleting city reports. Pressure updates on the same demo tank share a work item and retain source history. The facilities page shows equipment checks, work items and simulated teams. **Load city demo** provides the separate Downtown LA examples.

The header **Live call** shortcut opens capture in the selected workspace. Start the microphone, finish the call, review the transcript and event location, then add the incident. You don't need to create a session separately. A USC call needs a supported USC building before a campus team can be assigned; an unclear or out-of-area location remains for review.

**New test** saves and restarts the complete local demo run, including both workspaces. Saved tests can restore it. Transport settings, provider configuration and AI request budget are shared local demo controls. Equipment, pressure limits, teams and dispatch remain simulated. USC's geometry uses real OSM building footprints and outdoor paths; indoor exits and current gate access haven't been independently verified.
