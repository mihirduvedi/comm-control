# Voice to response demo

Open the desired world and choose **Start voice report** in the header. This is the shortest route to microphone transcription; creating a reporting session is optional when you want a separate caller page.

For City, say: “There’s smoke near City Hall. Two people are outside. I don’t know if anyone is still inside. Please send fire support.” For USC, say: “The demo tank in SGM reads 220 psi. Please send lab safety to check it.” These are fictional demonstration scripts.

Finish the call, review the transcript, extracted details and event location, then choose **Add reviewed incident**. Point out the transition: one voice report becomes a structured incident, a map location, a suitable available demo team and a route. The status strip above the map shows the real saved state of each step. If a team is busy or the location is unknown, it says so.

Open Response plan to inspect the assignment. Open Evidence to trace interpreted facts back to the caller’s exact words. Previously received blocked-road or campus-path reports affect later routes until a supported clearance or manual review reopens them. The route is a local preview, not live vehicle navigation.

Use the preloaded USC or City events to explain the control center without making a new model call. Those events are annotated demos; they do not demonstrate new voice inference. The four hosted browser tests remain explicit opt-ins. A live rehearsal needs Internet access, the configured provider and the user’s microphone/speakers.

Scroll outside the map to move the whole workspace. Scroll over the map to zoom. The incident queue scrolls separately for long lists and auto-scrolls while dragging at its top or bottom edge. Expand map provides a larger view; Escape restores the workspace.

Use Resources to show coverage by capability, destination and current state. USC also exposes fictional equipment conditions and their source-linked histories. New test saves and resets the entire local run across both worlds; restore a saved test to return to it.

All dispatch, teams, readings and response communications remain local or simulated. Report review remains part of the flow. No real emergency-service integration, indoor access certification or model-weight training is claimed.
